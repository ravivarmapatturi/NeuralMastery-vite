# Behavioral

Technical skill gets you shortlisted; behavioral rounds are frequently where offers are actually decided, especially when several candidates are technically comparable.

## The STAR Method

Structure every story as: **Situation** (brief context), **Task** (what you were responsible for), **Action** (what *you* specifically did — not "we"), **Result** (the outcome, ideally with a concrete metric or clear impact). The most common mistake is spending too long on Situation/Task and rushing Action/Result — the Action step is what the interviewer is actually trying to evaluate about you.

## Stories Worth Having Ready

- **A conflict with a teammate**: shows how you handle disagreement professionally — focus on how you resolved it, not on being "right."
- **A failed project**: shows self-awareness and what you learned — avoid stories that are secretly someone else's fault; own the part that was genuinely yours.
- **A time you influenced without authority**: shows leadership potential beyond formal title — relevant even for individual-contributor roles, since it demonstrates you can drive outcomes through persuasion and technical credibility, not just by being told what to do.

## Tailor to the Company

Research the specific company/team's ML maturity before the interview — a startup building its first ML pipeline cares about different things (scrappiness, working with ambiguity, wearing many hats) than a mature ML org with established infrastructure (working within existing systems, collaborating across specialized teams). Generic answers read as generic; answers that reference the team's actual context read as genuine interest.

## Practical Prep

Write out 5-6 core stories in advance (conflict, failure, leadership, a proud technical win, a time you changed your mind based on data) and practice adapting each one to different question phrasings — most behavioral questions are variations on a small number of underlying themes, so a handful of well-prepared stories covers far more ground than trying to have a unique story for every possible question.
